For a general kernel, write T_n=P_(I_n) C_f E_(I_(n-1)), where E extends by zero, C_f is discrete convolution, and P restricts the output. This explains the Toeplitz form without invoking a spectral theorem. Finite truncation makes every displayed product finite. Whenever the convolution sums are defined, global convolution preserves periodic sequences; the nontrivial constraints here come from zero extension and moving boundaries. Standard invariant-space theory alone does not remove those constraints. This is a mathematical comparison, not a claim that no broader theorem can imply them.

## Primary statements inspected again in Phase 5

The following actual definitions and theorem statements were read, rather than inferred from titles or abstracts. Full papers/proofs were not comprehensively reviewed.

1. Bailey, Cameron, Gavrilyuk and Goryainov, *Equitable partitions of Latin-square graphs*, arXiv:1802.01001v1, Section 2 before Proposition 2.1. The cell-indicator span is invariant exactly when the partition is equitable. Their setting is a fixed undirected graph; no spectral conclusion is transferred to rectangular transitions. This directly identifies our general preservation language as established background. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/1802.01001v1).

2. Coutinho and Godsil, *Graph Spectra and Continuous Quantum Walks*, preliminary notes dated 7 March 2021: title page and Chapter 3 identification checked. The notes are correctly identified as preliminary in our bibliography. Bailey supplies the specific invariant-space passage used above. No claim that quantum uniform mixing proves our counting rigidity is made. [Primary notes](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~cgodsil/quagmire/Fields24/pdfs/GrfSpc3.pdf).

3. Liu and Siemons, *Unlocking the walk matrix of a graph*, arXiv:1911.00062v3, Theorems 1.2, 5.1 and 5.5 and their displayed reconstruction arguments. These recover the adjacency matrix of a fixed finite simple undirected graph from a walk matrix of rank at least |V|−1. The starting vector can be the indicator of any vertex subset, not only the all-ones vector. Our changing rectangular transitions and translation-shared kernel are different data. The source establishes a strong prior reconstruction theme; difference of formulations is not evidence of originality. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/1911.00062v3).

4. Blåsten, Isozaki, Lassas and Lu, arXiv:2107.00494v2, Condition 1.4, Theorems 1.5–1.8 and observation definitions, PDF pages 6–9. Their finite graph, reversible transition model and Two-Points Condition support recovery from first-passing distributions on all pairs of observed vertices, or almost surely from one sufficiently observed realization. Conductance scale is a gauge; the transition matrix is recovered. Our origin endpoint rows in an expanding directed graph are not those measurements. No direct application to the boundary certificate is asserted. [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.00494v2).

5. De la Peña, Gzyl and McDonald, arXiv:math/0610821v1, Theorem 1.1, Definitions 2.2–2.3 and Theorem 2.4, PDF pages 2–4. A known rooted tree with a specified augmentation and known added-chain transitions is recovered from hitting-time/place distributions on two boundary shells. This is genuine recovery from one starting root, so a distinction based only on “one origin” would be misleading. The different graph, kernel and observation assumptions matter. [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0610821).

6. Lawler and Limic, *Random Walk: A Modern Introduction*, Section 12.4.1, equation (12.16) and Proposition 12.4.3 in the author-hosted PDF, printed pages 272–273. Restriction to a fixed set gives a substochastic killed matrix; under finite irreducibility/aperiodicity conditions, normalized powers have a positive asymptotic profile. Our windows change each step and the claim is exact equality, so this particular proposition is not the quantitative certificate. An expanded space-time representation does not automatically satisfy its recurrence/irreducibility assumptions. [Author-hosted text](https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~lawler/srwbook.pdf).
