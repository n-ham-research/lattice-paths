## New primary material actually inspected

Blåsten–Isozaki–Lassas–Lu, *Inverse problems for discrete heat equations and random walks for a class of graphs*, arXiv:2107.00494v2, 4 January 2023, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.00494v2 . Read introduction, Condition 1.4, Theorems 1.5–1.8 and their observation definitions (PDF pp.1–9), not the full proofs. Theorems 1.5–1.6 recover a finite connected reversible weighted graph from all first-passing distributions on an accessible set, or almost surely an infinite observed realization, under the Two-Points Condition. Our data are deterministic endpoint rows from one origin on expanding directed layers with a translation-invariant unknown kernel. Neither the observations nor hypotheses coincide; this is a comparison, not proof of nonoverlap with every consequence of their work. Phase 3 had only read the abstract; theorem statements are now checked.

De la Peña–Gzyl–McDonald, *Inverse problems for random walks on trees: network tomography*, arXiv:math/0610821v1, https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0610821v1 . Read introduction and Theorem 1.1 (PDF pp.1–3), not the full paper. With a known finite rooted tree augmented by suitable chains, joint hitting-time/place data on two boundary layers identify transition probabilities. Their reconstruction uses geometry and recursion. Our funnel result also exploits geometry, but observes full expanding rows and recovers time-independent jump weights; it does not improve their partial-observation theorem. The source establishes that recursive recovery from boundary data is already an old theme.
