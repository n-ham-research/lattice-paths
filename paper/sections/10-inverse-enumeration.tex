\section{Observation fibers and reconstruction}
For prescribed complete rows define $U=\bigcup_{n=1}^N(I_n-I_{n-1})$ and
\[
 A(p)_{(n,y),d}=\begin{cases}p_{n-1}(y-d)&y-d\in I_{n-1},\\0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
\]
The compatible field-valued kernels on $U$ are exactly $f+\ker A(p)$: necessity is the recurrence and sufficiency follows by induction on the prescribed rows. Thus a coordinate is identifiable exactly when its unit vector lies in the row space. Periodicity is determined precisely when a periodic compatible kernel exists and $\ker A(p)$ annihilates all within-residue differences. These are linear-algebra criteria, not new general inverse theory. Nonnegative or indicator fibers require intersection with the admissible set. Truly hidden intermediate rows instead give polynomial fibers; for example observing only $p_2(0)$ constrains $f(0)^2+f(1)f(-1)$.

\begin{theorem}[Labelled endpoint reconstruction]\label{thm:inverse}
For $N\ge1$, on triangular layers $I_n=\{0,\ldots,n\}$ over a field, complete labelled rows $p_0,\ldots,p_N$ determine the time-independent weights on $W_N=\{1-N,\ldots,N\}$ if
\[
p_{n-1}(0)\ne0,\quad p_{n-1}(n-1)\ne0\qquad(2\le n\le N).
\]
No weight outside $W_N$ is observable through that horizon. For ordinary integer counts (or their characteristic-zero image), $0,1\in D$ guarantee the pivots.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Initially $f(0)=p_1(0)$ and $f(1)=p_1(1)$. Given weights on $W_{n-1}$, the two boundary equations give
\begin{align*}
f(n)&=\frac{p_n(n)-\sum_{z=1}^{n-1}p_{n-1}(z)f(n-z)}{p_{n-1}(0)},\\
f(1-n)&=\frac{p_n(0)-\sum_{z=0}^{n-2}p_{n-1}(z)f(-z)}{p_{n-1}(n-1)}.
\end{align*}
All summands use known weights, proving induction. No transition through $N$ uses a difference outside $W_N$. All-zero and all-one increment words prove the indicator pivot condition for ordinary nonnegative counts. Over a positive-characteristic field, cancellation may instead make such a pivot zero.
\end{proof}
These are full-row data, not just two boundary sequences: the equations use previous interior counts. The argument is a specialized triangular elimination; compare fixed-graph walk-matrix recovery in \cite{LiuSiemons}. Nonextinction alone is insufficient: $D=\{0\}$ and $D=\Z_{\le0}$ produce the same entire array, supported only at height zero.

Nor does positivity transfer the structural finite horizon. For $q=5$, the step set $D=\{-5,-3,0,1,4,6\}$ has positive counts at every endpoint using $0,1$, and rows through $6$ are residue-uniform. Rows $5,6$ are
\[
(6,8,10,10,8,6),\qquad(22,22,24,20,24,22,22),
\]
but $f(-4)\ne f(1)$. Thus the old finite converse fails for actual counts even with full positive reachability. This is a finite-prefix obstruction; this example does not decide an all-horizon converse. Section~\ref{sec:compressed} proves specified small-modulus cases.

\begin{theorem}[Reconstruction on widening funnels]\label{thm:funnelinverse}
For $N\ge1$, $c\in\Z$, $I_n=[cn,cn+s_n]$ and $0=s_0\le\cdots\le s_N$, complete labelled rows determine $f$ on $[c-s_{N-1},c+s_N]$ if both preceding boundary counts are nonzero at every transition after the first. The coefficient domain is any field; the pivot condition is sufficient.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Shear to $c=0$. Row one recovers $f$ on $[0,s_1]$. At transition $n$ put $a=s_{n-2}$, $b=s_{n-1}$, $k=s_n$. The known window is $[-a,b]$. Use targets $y=b-a-1,\ldots,0$ in descending order to solve
\[
f(y-b)=\frac{p_n(y)-\sum_{z=0}^{b-1}p_{n-1}(z)f(y-z)}{p_{n-1}(b)}.
\]
All other arguments are known; this recovers $[-b,-a-1]$. Next use $y=b+1,\ldots,k$ in ascending order to solve the $z=0$ term with pivot $p_{n-1}(0)$. This recovers $[b+1,k]$. Empty blocks and plateaus cause no difficulty. The nested observable windows are exactly $[-s_{n-1},s_n]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Scalar observations identify kernel shape]\label{prop:scalarinverse}
For $N\ge1$ and a nonnegative real kernel on such a funnel, every actual row through $N$ is constant and nonzero if and only if $f=\alpha>0$ on $[c-s_{N-1},c+s_N]$. Then
\[
p_n(y)=\alpha^n\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(s_j+1).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each preceding row is a nonzero multiple of the constant vector, so constant actual output tests the whole one-dimensional constant-input space. Theorem~\ref{thm:funnels} forces the stated kernel shape; row one gives positivity. The reverse implication and formula follow by induction.
\end{proof}
For unbounded widths these qualitative observations force a globally constant kernel. This bridges actual rigidity and inverse identification; one row sum fixes $\alpha$. The argument does not transfer to a higher-dimensional residue space.

Compressed data also have an exact obstruction: reflection $(n,y)\mapsto(n,n-y)$ replaces $f(d)$ by $f(1-d)$ and reverses every row. Thus row multisets and totals cannot distinguish $D=\{0,1,2\}$ from $\{-1,0,1\}$, despite full positive reachability. Reflection need not be the only ambiguity. No finite-horizon data can certify finite support: adding jumps outside its observable window changes no observation. Related inverse random-walk results \cite[Theorems 1.5--1.6]{BlastenEtAl}\cite[Theorem 1.1]{DeLaPenaEtAl} use different fixed-graph and hitting-time data; recursive recovery itself is an established theme.
