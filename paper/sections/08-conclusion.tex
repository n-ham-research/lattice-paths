\section{Scope and further questions}
The finite rigidity theorem characterizes an entire invariant input space, with a sharp observable difference window. This supplies a structural result beyond the elementary factorial products, though priority and publication value remain to be established. The classical tree encodings unify several source models with explicit size statistics. Exact computations also reproduce all 697 printed entries of the original ten tables; two duplicated axis labels are recorded separately.

The next questions are whether other geometric layer families admit comparable converses, whether non-residue partitions yield useful bounded-dimensional quotients, and whether direct bijections explain the remaining dissection and algebraic identifications. Further source corrections and unsupported sequence alternatives remain in the research ledger. No improved factorial algorithm, completed novelty audit or submission-ready paper is claimed.
