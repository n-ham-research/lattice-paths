\begin{abstract}
We distinguish structural residue compression from uniformity along an actual counting trajectory. A boundary comparison graph characterizes weighted interval transitions preserving residue-constant inputs and yields finite triangular rigidity and scalar rigidity for arbitrary widening funnels. For actual trajectories, finite nonnegative jump support precludes nonextinct eventual residue uniformity on growing windows with sufficiently large predecessor cores, including rational cones. Complete labelled endpoint data recover the observable jump weights under two nonzero boundary pivots. A positive finite-horizon example shows why structural and trajectory converses differ. These independently proved results have unresolved historical priority; classical enumerations are supporting applications rather than novelty claims.
\end{abstract}
