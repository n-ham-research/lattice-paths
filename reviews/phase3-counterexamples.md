## Independent proof checks

For T1, the claimed interior interval is correct: y>=max(0,M) ensures y-d>=0; y<=n-max(0,1-m) ensures y-d<=n-1. A full block of q consecutive interior targets supplies every residue. Summing their q untruncated convolution equations counts each source residue exactly once per jump, so C_n=K C_(n-1). Meanwhile P_n<=KP_(n-1) follows from nonnegative weights and killing at the boundary. Row uniformity implies P_n>=floor((n+1)/q)C_n. Cancel K^(n-B), which is strictly positive since support is nonempty with positive total weight. This forces C_B=0 as the layer width diverges. Nonnegativity then makes the entire row zero. There is no hidden assumption of positivity at all endpoints, all residues being reached, or individual f(d) being 1. Empty support, signed weights, infinite support and fixed-width regions are correctly excluded from the argument.

For T1f, B>=q-1 supplies all residue cells in the first observed row, and B>=L+R+q-2 ensures every later observed transition contains q interior targets. The bounds floor((H+1)/q)C_H<=P_H and P_B<=ceil((B+1)/q)C_B give the strict contradiction exactly as written. Writing B+1=aq+r verifies H-B=q when r=0 and 2q-r otherwise; hence H<=B+2q-1, with at most 2q observed rows counting both endpoints. This is not an off-by-one horizon assertion. Uniformity before B is unnecessary. If the original trajectory had already vanished, the certificate holds trivially.

For T2, the new top difference n occurs only at source z=0; the new bottom difference 1-n occurs only at z=n-1. All other differences in either equation lie in the previously recovered window. The two nonzero pivot hypotheses therefore suffice over any field, even with signed weights and cancellations elsewhere. No difference outside [1-N,N] can enter a transition through N; the claimed unobservability is exact. The theorem accurately requires full labelled rows rather than only edge sequences. Its invisible-negative-jump example is valid: starting from zero, neither of the compared step sets ever reaches a positive height. For the unweighted q=1 consequence, first-row nonextinction and equality force both 0 and 1 present; the interior target y=1 at every n>=2 uses only already identified jumps. This yields n! and then both new jumps. Thus the induction does not assume the conclusion.

For the graph theorem, subtracting the two finite sums on each source residue cell cancels all internal terms in any ring's additive group. Its two surviving differences belong to the observable union even when that union is disconnected or has isolated vertices. Equalities along graph edges are necessary and sufficient. The converse using independent component weights requires a nonzero ring as qualified. The vector-space dimension statement follows without a rank assumption because the equations are precisely component equality constraints.

For funnels, at transition [0,M] to [0,K], K>=M, the graph edges are all pairs t,t+M+1 in the new window [-M,K]. Each residue class modulo M+1 is consequently a connected chain, possibly a singleton. The old connected interval [-s_(n-2),M] is nested and contains at least M+1 consecutive integers, so meets every chain. This proves connectedness for arbitrary width jumps, including zero widths and repeated widths. Shearing restores c exactly; the observable window is [c-s_(N-1),c+s_N]. The integer-cone theorem is a special case and its separate proof also works. Unbounded nondecreasing widths exhaust both ends of Z, justifying the infinite conclusion. No q>1 or nonmonotone extension has been inferred.
